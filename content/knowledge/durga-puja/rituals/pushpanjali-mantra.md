**Pushpanjali** (*pushpa*, flower; *anjali*, offering with cupped hands), usually just called **anjali**, is the moment during Durga Puja when ordinary devotees offer flowers to the goddess with Sanskrit mantras, repeating each phrase after the priest. It happens every day of the puja, but the **Maha Ashtami** morning anjali is the one most Bengalis never miss. In 2026 Maha Ashtami falls on Monday 19 October.

Below are the verses most commonly recited in Bengali pujas, each in Bengali script (as recited in Bengal), Devanagari, Roman transliteration and an English meaning. Priests' wording and the order of verses vary between families and pujas, so follow your priest when they differ from this page.

## How anjali is done

1. Wash your hands, and take off your shoes near the altar.
2. Take a few flowers and bel (wood apple) leaves from the plate passed around. Many people also receive a little sandalwood paste on the flowers.
3. Hold them in your cupped hands, palms together, in front of your chest.
4. Repeat each line after the priest, in a clear voice.
5. When the priest finishes the round with the offering line, offer the flowers towards the goddess's feet, usually into a basket or onto the altar, without throwing them at the image.
6. Take fresh flowers and repeat. Most pujas do **three rounds**.
7. Fold your hands for the closing pranam mantra, then receive shanti jal (sprinkled holy water) or prasad if offered.

## Verse 1: Jayanti Mangala Kali

This verse is from the **Argala Stotram**, a hymn recited with the Devi Mahatmya (Chandi). It is the most widely used anjali verse in Bengal.

> ওঁ জয়ন্তী মঙ্গলা কালী ভদ্রকালী কপালিনী।
> দুর্গা শিবা ক্ষমা ধাত্রী স্বাহা স্বধা নমোঽস্তু তে॥
> ॐ जयन्ती मङ्गला काली भद्रकाली कपालिनी । दुर्गा क्षमा शिवा धात्री स्वाहा स्वधा नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
> Om jayanti mangala kali bhadrakali kapalini, durga shiva kshama dhatri swaha swadha namo'stu te.

**Meaning:** "O Victorious One, Auspicious One, Kali, Bhadrakali, bearer of the skull; Durga, Benevolent One, Forgiving One, Sustainer of all; you who are Swaha and Swadha (the offerings made to the gods and to the ancestors): salutations to you."

In the standard Argala Stotram the second line reads *durga kshama shiva dhatri*, as in the Devanagari above. In Bengal it is commonly recited *durga shiva kshama dhatri*. Both are in use.

## The offering line

After the verse, each round ends with a formula that names what is being offered and to whom. A commonly recited form is:

> এষ সচন্দন পুষ্প বিল্বপত্রাঞ্জলি ওঁ শ্রী শ্রী দুর্গায়ৈ নমঃ।
> Esha sachandana pushpa bilwapatranjali, om shri shri durgayai namah.

**Meaning:** "This offering of flowers and bel leaves with sandalwood: Om, salutations to Sri Durga."

Priests vary this line. Some add a seed syllable (such as *hrim*) before the goddess's name, and many repeat the line for the other deities of the image or for the goddess "with her vehicle, weapons and companions".

## Verse 2: Mahishaghni Mahamaye

This prayer is commonly recited in a later round of the Bengali anjali.

> ওঁ মহিষঘ্নি মহামায়ে চামুণ্ডে মুণ্ডমালিনি।
> আয়ুরারোগ্যবিজয়ং দেহি দেবি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
> Om mahishaghni mahamaye chamunde mundamalini, ayur-arogya-vijayam dehi devi namo'stu te.

**Meaning:** "O slayer of Mahisha, great power of illusion, Chamunda, wearer of the garland of heads: grant long life, health and victory, O Goddess; salutations to you."

## Verse 3: Sarva Mangala Mangalye

These three verses come from the **Narayani Stuti** in chapter 11 of the Devi Mahatmya, where the gods praise the goddess after her victory. They are usually recited in the last round, or as the closing pranam.

> সর্বমঙ্গলমঙ্গল্যে শিবে সর্বার্থসাধিকে।
> শরণ্যে ত্র্যম্বকে গৌরি নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
> सर्वमङ्गलमाङ्गल्ये शिवे सर्वार्थसाधिके । शरण्ये त्र्यम्बके गौरि नारायणि नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
> Sarva mangala mangalye shive sarvartha sadhike, sharanye tryambake gauri narayani namo'stu te.

**Meaning:** "O auspiciousness of all that is auspicious, O Benevolent One who fulfils every aim, O refuge of all, three-eyed Gauri, Narayani: salutations to you."

> সৃষ্টিস্থিতিবিনাশানাং শক্তিভূতে সনাতনি।
> গুণাশ্রয়ে গুণময়ে নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
> Srishti sthiti vinashanam shakti bhute sanatani, gunashraye gunamaye narayani namo'stu te.

**Meaning:** "Eternal One, you are the power behind creation, preservation and dissolution; support of the qualities of nature and made of them, Narayani: salutations to you."

> শরণাগতদীনার্তপরিত্রাণপরায়ণে।
> সর্বস্যার্তিহরে দেবি নারায়ণি নমোঽস্তু তে॥
> Sharanagata dinarta paritrana parayane, sarvasyarti hare devi narayani namo'stu te.

**Meaning:** "You who are devoted to saving the humble and the suffering who come to you for refuge, Goddess who removes the pain of all, Narayani: salutations to you."

## A common order

Many Bengali pujas follow a pattern like this, though it is not universal:

| Round | Verse | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Om jayanti mangala kali | Esha sachandana pushpa bilwapatranjali |
| 2 | Om jayanti mangala kali, or Om mahishaghni mahamaye | Esha sachandana pushpa bilwapatranjali |
| 3 | Om jayanti mangala kali, then Sarva mangala mangalye and the other Narayani verses | Pranam and offering of flowers |

## Tips for Ashtami anjali

- Big pujas run several batches through the morning; ask when yours starts, as it depends on the Ashtami tithi that year.
- Arrive early: queues at popular pandals such as [Bagbazar](/guide/pandals/bagbazar/) can be long.
- Wear clothes you can stand in comfortably for a while; most people dress in new clothes for Ashtami.
