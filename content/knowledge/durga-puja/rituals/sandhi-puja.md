**Sandhi Puja** (*sandhi*, junction) is the worship of the goddess at the precise moment the **Ashtami** lunar day gives way to **Navami**. Traditionally it occupies **48 minutes**: the **last 24 minutes of Ashtami** and the **first 24 minutes of Navami**. The goddess is worshipped as **Chamunda**, and the offerings include **108 lotuses** and **108 lamps**. For many Bengalis it is the most powerful moment of the whole puja.

## When it happens

Hindu ritual days follow the *tithi*, the lunar day, not the calendar day. A tithi can begin and end at any hour, so Sandhi Puja may fall in the afternoon, late at night or before dawn, and on either 19 or 20 October in 2026. The exact time depends on the tithi junction in the panjika (almanac) the priest follows, and different almanacs can differ slightly. Ask your puja committee, or check a reliable local panjika; don't rely on a time you read for another year or another city.

## Chamunda and the junction

In the **Devi Mahatmya**, the goddess's fierce form **Chamunda** slays the demon generals **Chanda and Munda**, and takes her name from that victory. Bengali tradition places this battle at the junction of Ashtami and Navami, which is why the goddess is invoked as Chamunda at Sandhi Puja. Many households treat the junction with special solemnity: the pandal falls quiet before it, and when it arrives, the dhak, conch shells and ululation (*ulu*) rise together.

## 108 lotuses and 108 lamps

The best-known offerings are **108 lotuses** and **108 lamps** (*pradip*), lit and arranged before the goddess. 108 is a sacred number throughout Hindu worship, and in Bengal the lotus offering is tied to a story from the Ramayana.

### The story of the 108th lotus

In Krittibas Ojha's Bengali Ramayana, which differs from Valmiki's Sanskrit text here, Rama worships Durga in autumn before his final battle with Ravana, the *akal bodhon* or untimely awakening. He gathers 108 blue lotuses for her. To test him, the goddess hides one. Finding only 107, Rama, who was called "lotus-eyed", resolves to offer one of his own eyes in its place. As he raises his arrow, the goddess appears, stops him, and grants him victory. Sandhi Puja's 108 lotuses recall that devotion.

## Other offerings and older customs

Besides lotuses and lamps, offerings typically include flowers, bel leaves, fruit, sweets, rice and cooked food, according to the tradition of the puja. Historically some households and temples offered an animal sacrifice at Sandhi Puja; most pujas today do not, and many offer a symbolic substitute such as an ash gourd, sugarcane or banana. See [Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/) for more.

## Seeing Sandhi Puja in Kolkata

Old family pujas, such as [Sovabazar Rajbari](/guide/pandals/sovabazar_rajbari/), and community pujas such as [Bagbazar Sarbojanin](/guide/pandals/bagbazar/) perform it with full ceremony; see also [bonedi bari pujas](/durga-puja/bonedi-bari-pujas/). Pandals fill up well before the junction, so arrive early and be ready for crowds on [Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) night.
