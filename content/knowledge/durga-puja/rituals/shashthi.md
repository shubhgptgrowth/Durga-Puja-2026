**Shashthi**, the sixth day of Devi Paksha, is when the main Durga Puja formally begins. In the morning or by day the priest performs *Kalparambha*, the ritual start of the puja; in the evening comes the **bodhon** (awakening) of the goddess, followed by *amantran* (invitation) and *adhivas* (consecration). In 2026 Shashthi falls on **Saturday 17 October**.

As with every day of the puja, the exact sequence and timing depend on the family tradition and the panjika (almanac) the priest follows; some households perform parts of these rites on other days.

## Kalparambha: the vow to begin

*Kalparambha* means "beginning of the rite". The priest, on behalf of the household or puja committee, makes a formal resolve (*sankalpa*) to perform the worship over the coming days, often with a kalash (water pot) set up as a seat for the goddess. It is usually brief and attended mainly by the organisers.

## Bodhon: waking the goddess

The heart of Shashthi is the **bodhon**. In Hindu belief, the half of the year from the summer to the winter solstice is the gods' night, so a goddess worshipped in autumn must first be woken. Bengali retellings of the Ramayana, notably Krittibas's, say that Rama performed this *akal bodhon* (untimely awakening) before his battle with Ravana, and the autumn puja follows him.

The bodhon is traditionally performed beside a **bel tree** (wood apple, *Aegle marmelos*), sacred to Shiva and the goddess. In city pandals where no tree is at hand, a bel branch is set up in a pot. The priest invokes the goddess in the tree with mantras, conch shells and ululation (*ulu*), and the dhak drums play.

## Amantran and adhivas

After the bodhon the goddess is formally **invited** (*amantran*) to accept the worship of the coming days. Then comes **adhivas**, a consecration in which the priest touches the image and the bel branch with a series of auspicious items, such as earth, fragrance, stone, paddy, durba grass, flowers, fruit, curd, ghee, a mirror, vermilion, a conch and others. The list varies by tradition. Adhivas prepares the image and the plants that will become the Nabapatrika the next morning.

## The unveiling of faces

For visitors, the moment many remember is the **unveiling**. In a number of Kolkata pujas the face of the idol, covered since it arrived from [Kumartuli](/durga-puja/kumartuli-idol-making/), is uncovered on Shashthi evening. Others unveil earlier, especially pujas that open to the public on Panchami or before. Crowds start to build from Shashthi evening and keep growing through Navami; see [pandal hopping tips](/durga-puja/pandal-hopping-tips/) and the [dates page](/guide/dates/) for crowd forecasts.

## Shashthi fasting for children

In many Bengali families, mothers keep a fast or a restricted diet on this day for their children's wellbeing, connected with the goddess Shashthi, a guardian of children. Some eat no rice that day, some break the fast after worship with fruit and sweets. Customs differ, so follow your own family's practice.

## Panchami, the day before

**Panchami** (Friday 16 October 2026) has no major rite of the main puja in most traditions, but in Kolkata it is the unofficial first night of the festival: many pandals are already lit and open, and those who want to avoid the heaviest crowds often go pandal hopping then.

## What comes next

At dawn the next day, the [Saptami](/durga-puja/rituals/saptami/) rites begin with the bath of the [Kola Bou](/durga-puja/rituals/kola-bou/). For the full sequence, see [Durga Puja rituals day by day](/durga-puja/rituals/). Good places to feel the start of the festival include [Bagbazar](/guide/pandals/bagbazar/), [College Square](/guide/pandals/college_square/) and [Md. Ali Park](/guide/pandals/md_ali_park/) in the [North](/guide/areas/north/) and [Central](/guide/areas/central/) parts of the city.
