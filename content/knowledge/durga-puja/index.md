Durga Puja (*pujo* in everyday Bengali) is the great autumn festival of the goddess Durga. It celebrates her victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, and in Bengal it is just as much a family reunion: the goddess arrives as Uma, the daughter of the Himalaya, coming home to her parents for a few days with her children Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartik. For five days, from Shashthi to Bijoya Dashami, temples, family homes and thousands of neighbourhood *pandals* (temporary shrines) hold worship, food, music and art, and then the images are carried to the river and immersed.

## The rituals, day by day

The [rituals hub](/durga-puja/rituals/) walks through the whole sequence. Start with [Mahalaya](/durga-puja/rituals/mahalaya/), the new-moon day when the season begins and many Bengalis wake before dawn for the [Mahishasuramardini radio programme](/durga-puja/mahishasuramardini-radio/). Then follow [Shashthi](/durga-puja/rituals/shashthi/), [Saptami](/durga-puja/rituals/saptami/) with the bathing of the [Kola Bou](/durga-puja/rituals/kola-bou/), [Maha Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) with [Kumari Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/kumari-puja/) and [Sandhi Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/sandhi-puja/), [Maha Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/) and [Bijoya Dashami](/durga-puja/rituals/dashami/).

Some moments people search for most:

- The words of the morning flower offering: [Pushpanjali mantra](/durga-puja/rituals/pushpanjali-mantra/).
- The evening dance with incense burners: [Dhunuchi naach](/durga-puja/rituals/dhunuchi-naach/).
- The farewell of married women smearing vermilion: [Sindoor khela](/durga-puja/rituals/sindoor-khela/).
- The drummers who set the rhythm of the festival: [Dhak and dhaki](/durga-puja/dhak-and-dhaki/).
