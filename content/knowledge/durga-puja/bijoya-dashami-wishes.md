**Subho Bijoya** (শুভ বিজয়া, "auspicious victory") is what Bengalis say to each other once the goddess has been immersed on Bijoya Dashami, the final day of [Durga Puja](/durga-puja/). In 2026 that is **Wednesday 21 October**. The greeting is bittersweet: it celebrates Durga's victory over Mahishasura and also says goodbye until she returns next year. All the wishes on this page were written for Pujo Parikrama; copy and share them freely.

## The Bijoya custom

Bijoya is not just a phrase; it comes with gestures, food and visits. Customs differ by family, but these are widely shared:

- **Pranam.** Younger people touch the feet of elders, and the elders bless them, often with a hand on the head.
- **Kolakuli.** Friends, cousins and equals embrace, traditionally three times, alternating shoulders.
- **Mishti.** Sweets are everywhere: rosogolla, sandesh, narkel naru (coconut balls) and nimki (savoury crackers) are offered to every visitor. See our page on [Durga Puja food](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/).
- **Visits.** In the days after Dashami people visit relatives and neighbours they owe a Bijoya call, and some families keep this up for weeks.
- **Letters and calls.** Older generations wrote *Bijoyar chithi*, Bijoya letters, to relatives far away, often addressed to elders with a pranam at the top. Today the same greeting goes by phone, video call and message.

Before all this, married women take part in *sindoor khela* and *boron*, bidding the goddess farewell; read more on the [Dashami](/durga-puja/rituals/dashami/) and [Sindoor Khela](/durga-puja/rituals/sindoor-khela/) pages. As the immersion procession leaves, crowds traditionally shout *"Asche bochhor abar hobe!"* (আসছে বছর আবার হবে, "it will happen again next year").

## Subho Bijoya wishes in Bengali

Each wish is given in Bengali script, then in English meaning.

1. শুভ বিজয়ার প্রণাম ও ভালোবাসা নিও।
   *Accept my Bijoya pranam and my love.*
2. মা আবার আসবেন, ততদিন ভালো থেকো, হাসিখুশি থেকো।
   *Ma will come again; until then, stay well and stay happy.*
3. মায়ের আশীর্বাদে তোমার সারা বছর হাসি আর মিষ্টিতে ভরে থাকুক।
   *With Ma's blessing, may your whole year be full of smiles and sweets.*
4. ঢাকের আওয়াজ থেমে গেল, কিন্তু মনের আনন্দ থাকুক সারা বছর।
   *The dhak has gone quiet, but may the joy in your heart last all year.*
5. বড়দের প্রণাম, ছোটদের ভালোবাসা। শুভ বিজয়া!
   *Pranam to the elders, love to the little ones. Subho Bijoya!*
6. শুভ বিজয়ার আন্তরিক শুভেচ্ছা ও অভিনন্দন।
   *Heartfelt Bijoya greetings and good wishes.* (formal)

## Sharodiya shubhechha: wishes before the puja

Use these from Mahalaya (Saturday 10 October 2026) until Navami.

1. শারদীয়ার আন্তরিক শুভেচ্ছা।
   *Heartfelt Sharodiya (autumn festival) greetings.*
2. মা আসছেন! শারদীয়ার শুভেচ্ছা রইল।
   *Ma is coming! Sharodiya greetings to you.*
3. শারদ উৎসব আনন্দে কাটুক, মা দুর্গা সবার মঙ্গল করুন।
   *May the autumn festival pass in joy, and may Ma Durga bless everyone.*

## Subho Bijoya wishes in English

1. Subho Bijoya! May Ma Durga's strength stay with you long after the dhak goes quiet.
2. The pandals come down, the memories stay. Wishing you a joyful Bijoya and a year of good things.
3. Victory to the goddess, and peace and sweetness to your home. Subho Bijoya.
4. Happy Durga Puja! May every evening of the festival bring you light, laughter and good food.

## Durga Puja wishes in Hindi

1. शुभ विजयादशमी! माँ दुर्गा आपके जीवन में सुख, शांति और समृद्धि लाएँ।
   *Happy Vijayadashami! May Ma Durga bring happiness, peace and prosperity to your life.*
2. दुर्गा पूजा की हार्दिक शुभकामनाएँ। माँ का आशीर्वाद सदा आप पर बना रहे।
   *Warm Durga Puja wishes. May Ma's blessing always remain with you.*
3. बुराई पर अच्छाई की जीत का यह पर्व आपके घर में नई रोशनी लाए।
   *May this festival of good over evil bring new light to your home.*
4. माँ अगले बरस फिर आएँगी, तब तक मुस्कुराते रहिए। शुभ बिजया!
   *Ma will come again next year; keep smiling until then. Subho Bijoya!*

## Tips for sending wishes

- Say **Subho Bijoya** only after the immersion; before that, use **Sharodiya shubhechha** or "Happy Durga Puja".
- For an elder, add *pranam*; to a younger person, *ashirbad* (blessings) or *bhalobasha* (love).
- A call or a visit with a box of sweets still means more than a forwarded image.
