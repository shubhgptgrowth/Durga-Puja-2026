For a simple Durga Puja at home you need a **ghat or kalash** (water pot) with mango leaves, a **picture or small image** of Ma Durga, a **lamp**, **incense**, **sindoor** and **sandalwood paste**, **red hibiscus** and **bel leaves**, **uncooked rice**, **durva grass**, and **fruit and sweets** for the offering. Below is a fuller checklist, grouped so you can shop once and tick things off. It is written for household worship; a priest-led full Durga Puja uses a much longer list, which the *purohit* (priest) will usually give the family.

Families and regions differ. A Bengali home, a Gujarati home and a Punjabi home preparing for Navratri will have overlapping but different lists. Treat this as a reliable starting point, and add or drop items according to your custom. For how to use these items, see [how to do Durga Puja at home](/durga-puja/at-home/).

## Essentials

| Item | Bengali / common name | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Picture or small image of Ma Durga | Pratima / chhobi | Focus of worship |
| Pot of brass, copper or clay | Ghat / kalash | Seat of the Goddess's presence |
| Five mango leaves | Aamer pallab | Placed in the ghat's mouth |
| Green coconut or whole fruit | Dab / phal | Rests on the ghat |
| Uncooked sun-dried rice | Atap chal | Seat for the ghat, offerings, sankalpa |
| Red or yellow cloth | Lal kapor | Covering the stool or altar |
| Low wooden stool | Jolchouki / chowki | Altar |
| Sitting mat | Asan | For the person doing the puja |
| Ganga water or clean water | Gangajal | Purifying and filling the ghat |
| Sindoor | Sindur | Swastika on the ghat, offering |
| Sandalwood paste | Chandan | Gandha (fragrance) offering |
| Lamp with ghee or mustard oil, wicks | Prodip / diya | Light and aarti |
| Incense sticks or resin | Dhup / dhuno | Dhoop offering |
| Bell | Ghanta | Rung during offerings |
| Conch (optional) | Shankha | Blown at aarti and auspicious moments |
| Red thread | Lal suto / mauli | Tied round the ghat or wrist |
| Matches, small plates, a bowl and spoon | Kosha-kushi (copper vessel and spoon), if available | Offering water |
| Chandi Path book or recording | Chandi / Durga Saptashati | Recitation |

## Flowers and leaves

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Red hibiscus (jaba) | The flower most associated with Durga in Bengal |
| Lotus (padma) | Prized for Sandhi Puja; a few are enough at home |
| Marigold (genda) | For garlands and decoration |
| White flowers (e.g. tuberose, jasmine) | Widely used; customs about specific flowers vary |
| Bel (bael) leaves | Offered with flowers, especially at anjali; three-leaflet sprigs are preferred |
| Durva grass | Offered with rice; also used for Ganesha |
| Tulsi | Some families offer it; many do not offer tulsi to Devi, so follow your custom |
| Mango leaves | For the ghat and for a *toran* (door hanging) |
| Banana leaf | To serve bhog on, if you like |

## Offerings and bhog

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Seasonal fruit | Banana, apple, guava, sugarcane pieces, cucumber, whatever is fresh |
| Sweets | Sandesh, batasha, narkel naru (coconut balls) |
| Milk and curd | For *payesh* (kheer) or offering as is |
| Honey, ghee, sugar | With milk and curd these make *panchamrit* |
| Betel leaf and areca nut | Paan-supari, a classic offering |
| Bhog ingredients | Gobindobhog rice and moong dal for khichuri, vegetables for labra |
| Drinking water | Kept with every food offering |

See [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) for what each day's bhog usually looks like.

## For Ashtami anjali

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Extra flowers and bel leaves | Enough for each person to offer three times |
| A plate or basket | To hold and collect offered flowers |
| Clean water | Sprinkled before anjali |
| Printed or saved mantra | The [pushpanjali mantra](/durga-puja/rituals/pushpanjali-mantra/) with transliteration |

## For Dashami

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Curd and flattened rice | *Doi-chire*, offered in many Bengali homes before farewell |
| Sweets | For offering and Bijoya greetings |
| Paan (betel leaf) | Offered to the Goddess; also used in *boron* (farewell) at pandals |
| Sindoor | For [sindoor khela](/durga-puja/rituals/sindoor-khela/) |
| A mirror (optional) | Some families show the Goddess's reflection in water or a mirror at visarjan |
| A pot of soil or a plant | To receive the ghat water and flowers respectfully |

## Extra items for Navratri

If you follow the Navratri tradition, add: a wide clay tray, clean soil and **barley (jau)** seeds for the sprouting pot, a red *chunri* (scarf) for the Goddess, and the ingredients for [Navratri fasting food](/navratri/fasting-rules/) and [kanya pujan](/navratri/kanya-pujan/). The [ghatasthapana guide](/navratri/ghatasthapana/) explains how they fit together.

## Substitutions that are fine at home

- **No mango leaves?** Use betel or ashoka leaves, which many families also treat as auspicious.
- **No coconut?** A whole fruit, or a small plate of rice, can cover the ghat.
- **No brass pot?** A clean clay pot is traditional and inexpensive; any clean pot is acceptable to many families.
- **No Ganga water?** Clean water with sincere intention is what matters most.
- **No hibiscus?** Any fresh, fragrant flower that hasn't been smelled or worn first.
- **No sandalwood?** A little sindoor or turmeric paste.
- **Can't read Sanskrit?** Read the Chandi in Bengali, Hindi or English, or listen to it.

## Buying and disposal tips

- Buy flowers the day before or the same morning; keep them in water, covered with a damp cloth.
- Choose clay lamps and natural cloth over plastic.
- After Dashami, return flowers, leaves and the ghat water to soil or a garden rather than throwing them in a river or a bin. Some neighbourhoods and pandals collect puja flowers for composting; ask locally.
