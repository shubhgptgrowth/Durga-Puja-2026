The short answer: **Durga Puja 2026 runs from Shashthi, Saturday 17 October, to Bijoya Dashami, Wednesday 21 October, with Mahalaya on Saturday 10 October.** It celebrates the goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura and, in Bengal, her homecoming. Below are 40 short answers to the questions people ask most. Each links to an in-depth guide; for the full calendar see [Durga Puja 2026 dates](/guide/dates/), and for unfamiliar words the [glossary](/durga-puja/glossary/).

## Dates and basics

### When is Durga Puja in 2026?

Mahalaya is Saturday 10 October 2026, Panchami Friday 16 October, Shashthi Saturday 17 October, Saptami Sunday 18 October, Maha Ashtami Monday 19 October, Maha Navami Tuesday 20 October and Bijoya Dashami Wednesday 21 October. See [all 2026 dates](/guide/dates/).

### Why do Durga Puja dates change every year?

The festival follows the Hindu lunar calendar, falling in the bright fortnight of the month of Ashwin, so its Gregorian dates move between September and October. Exact ritual timings come from the panjika (almanac) and can differ slightly between almanacs.

### What is Durga Puja?

It is the Hindu festival worshipping the goddess Durga, the embodiment of divine power (Shakti), above all in West Bengal, Assam, Odisha, Tripura, Bihar, Jharkhand and among Bengalis worldwide. Read [What is Durga Puja?](/durga-puja/what-is-durga-puja/).

### Why is Durga Puja celebrated?

It celebrates Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, a story told in the Devi Mahatmya. In Bengal she is also welcomed as Uma, a daughter returning to her parents' home for a few days. See [the Mahishasura story](/durga-puja/mahishasura-story/).

### How many days does Durga Puja last?

The main festival is five days, Shashthi to Bijoya Dashami, but the season begins with Mahalaya about a week earlier, and many pandals open to visitors from Panchami or before.

### What is Mahalaya?

Mahalaya is the new-moon day that ends the fortnight of remembering ancestors and marks the start of Devi Paksha, the goddess's fortnight. Many Bengalis wake before dawn to listen to the radio programme *Mahishasuramardini*. See [Mahalaya](/durga-puja/rituals/mahalaya/) and [the Mahishasuramardini broadcast](/durga-puja/mahishasuramardini-radio/).

### What is akal bodhon?

*Akal bodhon* means "untimely awakening": the story, told in Krittibas's Bengali Ramayana, that Rama invoked Durga in autumn, out of season, before his battle with Ravana. Bengali tradition links the autumn puja to it. Read [Akal Bodhon](/durga-puja/akal-bodhon/).

### Who are the figures in a Durga Puja idol?

Durga stands on her lion, killing Mahishasura, flanked by her children Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesh and Kartik, each with their animal mount. Ganesh's companion, the *kola bou*, stands beside him. See [Goddess Durga](/durga-puja/goddess-durga/).

### Is Durga Puja a UNESCO heritage?

Yes. UNESCO inscribed Durga Puja in Kolkata on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2021. Read [Durga Puja and UNESCO](/durga-puja/unesco-heritage/).

### How old is Durga Puja?

Worship of the goddess is ancient, and large family pujas in Bengal are recorded from several centuries ago; the community or *sarbojanin* puja grew in the twentieth century. See the [history of Durga Puja](/durga-puja/history/).

## Durga Puja and Navratri

### What is the difference between Durga Puja and Navratri?

Both fall in the same autumn period and honour the Goddess. Navratri is nine nights of worship of her nine forms, with fasting and, in western India, garba and dandiya; Bengal's Durga Puja centres on the last five days, with clay idols of Durga and her children. See [Navratri](/navratri/).

### Are Dussehra and Bijoya Dashami the same day?

They fall on the same day, the tenth day of the bright fortnight. In much of north India it is Dussehra, marking Rama's victory over Ravana; in Bengal it is Bijoya Dashami, the goddess's farewell. In 2026 it is Wednesday 21 October.

### Is Durga Puja celebrated outside Bengal?

Yes, across India and wherever Bengali communities live, from Delhi and Mumbai to London, New York and beyond. See [Durga Puja around the world](/durga-puja/around-the-world/) and our guide to other [festivals](/festivals/).

## Rituals

### What happens on Shashthi?

The goddess is formally welcomed with *bodhon* (awakening), *amantran* (invitation) and *adhibas* (preparatory rites), and most pujas unveil the idol's face. See [Shashthi](/durga-puja/rituals/shashthi/).

### What is kola bou?

The *kola bou* ("banana bride") is a banana plant bound with eight other plants and draped in a red-bordered sari, bathed at dawn on Saptami and placed beside Ganesh. It represents the goddess in plant form. Read [Kola Bou](/durga-puja/rituals/kola-bou/).

### What is pushpanjali?

*Pushpanjali* or *anjali* is the offering of flowers to the goddess while repeating Sanskrit mantras after the priest, most famously on Ashtami morning; many people fast until they have offered it. See [the pushpanjali mantra](/durga-puja/rituals/pushpanjali-mantra/).

### What is Sandhi Puja?

It is worship at the junction (*sandhi*) of the Ashtami and Navami lunar days, traditionally 48 minutes, with 108 lotuses and 108 lamps. Its clock time changes each year, so check the panjika. Read [Sandhi Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/sandhi-puja/).

### What is Kumari Puja?

The worship of a young girl as a living form of the goddess, usually on Maha Ashtami; the best-known public one is at Belur Math near Kolkata. See [Kumari Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/kumari-puja/).

### What happens on Maha Navami?

Navami brings the final full day of worship, with the *homa* (fire offering) at many pujas and an emotional last night of pandal hopping. See [Maha Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/).

### What happens on Bijoya Dashami?

The goddess is bid farewell: the *darpan bisarjan* (her reflection in a mirror in water), *sindoor khela*, immersion processions and Bijoya greetings. See [Bijoya Dashami](/durga-puja/rituals/dashami/).

### What is sindoor khela?

On Dashami, women offer sindoor (vermilion) and sweets to the goddess, then smear sindoor on each other. Traditionally it is for married women; some pujas now welcome a wider group, so ask locally. Read [Sindoor Khela](/durga-puja/rituals/sindoor-khela/).

### What is dhunuchi naach?

A devotional dance with a *dhunuchi*, a clay incense burner of smouldering coconut husk and resin, performed to the dhak during the evening aarti. See [Dhunuchi Naach](/durga-puja/rituals/dhunuchi-naach/).

### What is the dhak?

The *dhak* is the large barrel drum played by *dhakis* throughout the puja; its rhythm is the sound of the festival. Read [Dhak and dhaki](/durga-puja/dhak-and-dhaki/).

### What is bisarjan?

*Bisarjan* is the immersion of the idol in a river or pond, symbolising the goddess's return to Kailash. It begins on Dashami and continues for some days at some pujas; local authorities set immersion arrangements. See [eco-friendly Durga Puja](/durga-puja/eco-friendly-puja/).

### What is a bonedi bari puja?

A puja held by an old, established Kolkata family, often for generations, usually in the family's own *thakur dalan* (worship hall). Read [Bonedi bari pujas](/durga-puja/bonedi-bari-pujas/).

### Where are the idols made?

Many of Kolkata's idols are made in Kumartuli, the potters' quarter in north Kolkata, from straw, bamboo and river clay. Read [Kumartuli idol making](/durga-puja/kumartuli-idol-making/).

## Food

### What is bhog?

*Bhog* is food offered to the goddess and then shared as blessed food, typically khichuri, labra (mixed vegetables), fries, chutney and payesh. See [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) and our [recipes](/durga-puja/recipes/).

### Do Bengalis eat non-vegetarian food during Durga Puja?

Many do. Bhog offered to the goddess is usually vegetarian, but outside the ritual many Bengali families enjoy fish and meat during the puja days. Customs vary by family; some keep certain days vegetarian.

### What should I eat in Kolkata during the pujas?

Phuchka, rolls, mughlai paratha, biryani, telebhaja (fritters) and sweets such as rosogolla and sandesh, plus bhog where it is offered to visitors. See the [food guide](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/).

### What do people say on Bijoya Dashami?

"Shubho Bijoya" is the usual greeting. People touch elders' feet, embrace friends and share sweets in the days after Dashami. See [Bijoya Dashami wishes](/durga-puja/bijoya-dashami-wishes/).

## Etiquette and dress

### Can non-Hindus and tourists visit pandals?

Yes. Public pandals welcome everyone. Dress modestly, remove shoes where asked, don't touch the idol or ritual items, and follow volunteers' directions.

### Do I need to remove my shoes?

At many family pujas and near the altar in some pandals, yes; in others you can walk through with shoes on. Follow the signs and what others do, and wear easy slip-ons.

### Can I take photos at a pandal?

Usually yes in public pandals, but some family pujas restrict photography, and flash is unwelcome during puja and aarti. Ask before photographing people. See [photography tips](/durga-puja/photography-tips/).

### What should I wear for Durga Puja?

New clothes are traditional: saris, kurtas or dhuti-panjabi, in breathable fabrics for the October heat, plus comfortable shoes. See [what to wear for Durga Puja](/durga-puja/what-to-wear/).

## Visiting Kolkata

### What is the best time for pandal hopping?

Early morning, from about dawn to 9 am, and late night after about 1 am. Evenings from about 6 pm to midnight, Saptami to Navami, are the busiest. See [pandal hopping tips](/durga-puja/pandal-hopping-tips/).

### How should I get around Kolkata during the pujas?

By Metro and on foot, mostly. Roads near big pandals are often closed or jammed in the evenings; app cabs and autos help for short hops. See the [Kolkata traveller's guide](/durga-puja/visit-kolkata/).

### Where should I stay in Kolkata for Durga Puja?

Near a Metro station. North and central Kolkata suit heritage and blockbuster pandals, south Kolkata the classic neighbourhood pujas, and Salt Lake or New Town are calmer. Book early.

### Which are the best pandals in Kolkata?

It depends on what you like: heritage pujas in the north, giant theme pandals in central and south Kolkata, or classic neighbourhood pujas. Browse by area and trail in our [Kolkata pandal guide](/guide/), which shows live crowd check-ins.

### Is Durga Puja suitable for children and elderly visitors?

Yes, with planning: go at quieter hours, see fewer pandals, give children a contact card, and ask about separate entrances. See [Durga Puja with kids and elderly visitors](/durga-puja/with-kids-and-elderly/).

### Can I celebrate Durga Puja at home?

Yes. Many families hold a simple home puja with a small idol, picture or sacred pot, flowers and bhog. See [Durga Puja at home](/durga-puja/at-home/) and the [samagri list](/durga-puja/samagri-list/).
