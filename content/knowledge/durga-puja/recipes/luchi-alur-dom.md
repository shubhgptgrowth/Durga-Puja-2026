On [Maha Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) morning, many Bengali homes smell of hot oil and ginger. Family members who have fasted since dawn come back from offering *pushpanjali* (flower offering) at the pandal to a plate of puffed white luchi and a bowl of alur dom, a potato curry rich with spices. It is one of the most loved festive breakfasts in Bengal, and this version is made without onion or garlic, as many families prefer on puja days.

## Its place in the puja

In many households Ashtami is a day of *niramish* (vegetarian food without onion or garlic), and some families avoid rice until the main puja is done. Luchi, made from refined flour and fried in ghee or oil, is festive and rice-free, which makes it a natural choice. Pushpanjali is offered in the morning, and once it is over, families gather for this meal. Some eat luchi again on [Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/), or with [labra](/durga-puja/recipes/labra/) or *chholar dal* (Bengal gram dal) instead of alur dom. Practices differ widely, so take this as one common pattern rather than a rule. In 2026, Maha Ashtami falls on Monday 19 October. For the bhog meal later in the day, see our [Durga Puja bhog recipes](/durga-puja/recipes/).

## Tips for success

- **Firm dough, rested.** The dough should be firmer than chapati dough. Resting lets the gluten relax so it rolls out without shrinking back.
- **Oil, not flour, for rolling.** Dry flour falls off into the frying oil and burns, darkening every luchi after it.
- **Even thickness.** A luchi that is thick in the middle and thin at the edges will not puff fully. Roll gently from the centre outwards, turning as you go.
- **Hot oil, one at a time.** Fry each luchi separately and press it gently under the oil so the steam inside puffs it up.
- **Pale is right.** Traditional luchi are white or barely golden. If they brown quickly, lower the heat slightly.
- **Prick the potatoes.** Pricking boiled potatoes lets the gravy seep in, the "dom" in alur dom, which refers to slow cooking under cover.
- **Cook out the spices.** Let the ground spices fry in oil until the oil separates; raw-tasting spices are the most common problem with alur dom.

## Variations

- **Koraishutir kochuri:** stuff the luchi with a spiced green pea paste for a winter version.
- **Without tomato:** leave out the tomatoes and add 2 tbsp whisked yoghurt off the heat, or simply more water, for a drier alur dom.
- **Ghee luchi:** fry in ghee for an extra-rich festive luchi, as some families do on special days.
- **With onion and garlic:** for non-puja days, many cooks start the gravy with sliced onion and garlic paste.

## Storing

Luchi do not keep well and are best fried just before eating. The dough can be made 2–3 hours ahead and kept covered at room temperature. Alur dom keeps in the fridge for up to 2 days and tastes even better the next day; reheat with a splash of water.

## Serving

Serve 4–5 luchi per person with a bowl of alur dom, a spoonful of [tomato chutney](/durga-puja/recipes/tomato-khejur-chutney/) and, if you like, a hot [beguni](/durga-puja/recipes/beguni/) or a sweet such as rosogolla. Eat them the moment they come out of the oil.
