Durga Puja bhog is food cooked for the goddess, offered during the puja and then shared with everyone present as *prasad*, food that carries her blessing. The best-known version is the Ashtami bhog thali: soft, ghee-scented khichuri, a mixed vegetable labra, crisp beguni, a sweet-sour tomato chutney and a small bowl of payesh. The recipes on this page let you cook that thali at home, whether you are offering it at a home puja or simply want the taste of the pandal kitchen.

## What bhog is

*Bhog* (offering) is the cooked meal placed before the deity at a set time in the day's puja, usually around midday. Once the priest has offered it, it is no longer just lunch: it is prasad, and it is served to family, neighbours and visitors, often sitting in rows on the floor and eating off sal-leaf plates. At community pujas in Kolkata, volunteers cook bhog in huge pots for hundreds of people, and the queue for a plate on Ashtami is part of the festival. You can read more about this in our guide to [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/).

## The classic Ashtami bhog thali

Each dish on the thali has a job. Together they balance soft and crisp, savoury and sweet.

| Dish | Role on the plate | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| Bhoger khichuri | The main dish: gobindobhog rice and roasted moong dal with ghee | [Bhoger khichuri](/durga-puja/recipes/bhoger-khichuri/) |
| Labra | A soft mixed vegetable side, seasoned with panch phoron | [Labra](/durga-puja/recipes/labra/) |
| Beguni | Crisp, hot fritters for texture | [Beguni](/durga-puja/recipes/beguni/) |
| Chutney | Sweet and tangy, eaten towards the end | [Tomato khejur aamsotto chutney](/durga-puja/recipes/tomato-khejur-chutney/) |
| Payesh | The sweet finish | [Gobindobhog payesh](/durga-puja/recipes/payesh/) |

Families add or swap dishes freely. Some serve *chorchori* (a drier mixed vegetable) instead of labra, add a *bhaja* (fry) of potato or pumpkin, or replace payesh with a shop-bought sweet. Bonedi bari (old family) pujas often keep menus that have been handed down for generations. Treat this thali as typical rather than fixed.

## The no-onion, no-garlic convention

In many Bengali households, food offered to the goddess is cooked without onion or garlic, which are considered unsuitable for offerings. Flavour comes instead from ghee, ginger, cumin, bay leaf, dried red chilli, *panch phoron* (a five-seed mix of fenugreek, nigella, cumin, black mustard and fennel) and a pinch of *hing* (asafoetida). Every recipe in this section is written that way, including the [luchi and alur dom](/durga-puja/recipes/luchi-alur-dom/) for Ashtami morning. Some families also avoid tomatoes or certain vegetables in bhog; others are relaxed about it. If you are cooking for a home puja, ask whoever leads it what your family follows.

## Cooking with devotion

Bhog is cooked with care as well as skill. Common household customs include:

- Bathing and wearing clean clothes before entering the kitchen, and cooking in a freshly cleaned space.
- Using separate, clean utensils kept for puja cooking where possible.
- Keeping bhog apart from food that has already been eaten from, and not serving anyone before the offering is made.
- Not tasting the food before it is offered, because the first taste belongs to the goddess. Many cooks season by measurement and experience for this reason. Other families do not follow this rule, and tasting customs vary widely.

None of this needs to be elaborate. A clean kitchen, a calm mind and attention to the food are at the heart of it. If you are planning a full home puja, our [Durga Puja at home](/durga-puja/at-home/) guide and [samagri list](/durga-puja/samagri-list/) cover the rest.

## Planning the cooking

If you are cooking the full thali for a home puja on [Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) or [Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/), work backwards from the offering time. The chutney and naru can be made a day ahead. Payesh can be made in the early morning and left to cool. Start the labra and khichuri about an hour and a half before the offering, and fry the beguni last so it is still crisp when the plate goes before the goddess.
