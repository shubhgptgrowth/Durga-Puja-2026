Payesh is the sweet note that closes the Durga Puja bhog plate. After [khichuri](/durga-puja/recipes/bhoger-khichuri/), [labra](/durga-puja/recipes/labra/) and [chutney](/durga-puja/recipes/tomato-khejur-chutney/) comes a small bowl of creamy rice cooked in milk, scented with bay leaf and cardamom. It is one of the oldest sweets in Bengali cooking and, with only a few ingredients, depends on patience more than skill.

## Its place in the puja

Milk, rice and sugar are among the purest foods in Hindu offering traditions, and payesh appears at many auspicious moments in Bengali life: a child's first rice ceremony, birthdays, and the bhog on [Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) and [Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/). At community pujas it is often cooked in large brass or aluminium pots and served in small portions at the end of the meal. In homes, some families offer payesh in a separate bowl alongside the main bhog. Read more in our [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) guide.

## Tips for success

- **Use full-fat milk.** Low-fat milk gives a thin, watery payesh. Full-fat cow's milk reduces to a rich, naturally creamy base.
- **Choose a wide, heavy pan.** A wider surface reduces the milk faster, and a thick base helps stop scorching. A non-stick pan is a good choice if you are new to it.
- **Keep the ratio.** About 50 g of rice to 1 litre of milk gives a pourable payesh. More rice makes it thick and stodgy once it cools.
- **Cook the rice fully before adding sugar.** Sugar slows the softening of rice. If you add it too early, the grains stay firm.
- **Stir and scrape.** Run the spoon along the bottom and the sides of the pan; the skin that forms on the sides can be scraped back in for extra richness.
- **Remember it thickens.** Turn off the heat while the payesh still looks a little thinner than you want.

## The nolen gur variation

In winter, from around November to January, Bengal's markets fill with *nolen gur*, the new season's date-palm jaggery. *Nolen gurer payesh* is made with it in place of sugar and has a deep, smoky, caramel flavour. Since Durga Puja normally comes before the season, this is a version for later in the year. Cook the rice in milk as above, turn off the heat, wait 5 minutes and stir in about ½ cup of grated gur (or 3–4 tbsp liquid *jhola gur*). Do not boil it again, or the milk may split.

## Other variations

- **Saffron:** a few strands soaked in warm milk, added with the sugar, give a pale gold colour.
- **Less sweet:** reduce the sugar to ¼ cup (50 g) if you prefer, or if you are serving it alongside other sweets.
- **Thickened milk:** for a richer festive payesh, reduce 1.25 litres of milk for 10 minutes before adding the rice.
- **Nuts:** pistachios or almonds can replace cashews.

## Storing and serving

Payesh keeps in a covered container in the fridge for up to 3 days. It sets thicker when cold; stir in a few spoons of warm milk to loosen it. Many people love it chilled on a warm October afternoon, while others prefer it just warm. Serve in small bowls after the meal, or as part of the bhog thali with [narkel naru](/durga-puja/recipes/narkel-naru/) or another sweet alongside.
