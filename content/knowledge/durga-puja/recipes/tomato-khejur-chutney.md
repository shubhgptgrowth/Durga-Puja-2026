In a Bengali meal, chutney is not a condiment on the side but a course of its own, eaten near the end, after the savoury dishes and before the sweet. On the Durga Puja bhog plate it is usually a glossy red tomato chutney, sweet, gently sour and studded with dates and chewy pieces of dried mango. This version keeps the recipe simple and reliable.

## Its place in the puja

After the [khichuri](/durga-puja/recipes/bhoger-khichuri/), [labra](/durga-puja/recipes/labra/) and [beguni](/durga-puja/recipes/beguni/), a spoonful of chutney clears the palate before the [payesh](/durga-puja/recipes/payesh/). At community pujas it is often cooked the day before in large batches, since it keeps well and tastes better once the flavours have settled. Because it is made without onion or garlic, it fits easily into bhog cooking. Some families avoid tomatoes in offerings and make a chutney of raw mango, pineapple or papaya instead, so follow your household's custom. Our [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) guide has more on the bhog plate.

## Tips for success

- **Use very ripe tomatoes.** Deep red, soft tomatoes give colour and natural sweetness. Pale, firm tomatoes make a sharper, thinner chutney.
- **Balance sweet and sour.** Tomatoes vary a lot in acidity. If yours are very sour, add 1–2 tbsp more sugar; if they are sweet, a squeeze of lime at the end adds brightness.
- **Do not over-reduce.** The sugar sets the chutney as it cools. Stop when it just coats a spoon, otherwise it turns jammy and stiff.
- **Cut the aamsotto small.** Small pieces soften and scatter through the chutney instead of clumping.
- **Make it ahead.** Cooking the chutney a day in advance frees up the stove on puja day, and the flavour improves overnight.

## Variations

- **Ginger-forward:** double the ginger for a warmer, spicier chutney.
- **Cashews:** stir in a few broken cashews with the dates.
- **Plastic chutney:** for a festive alternative, peel and seed a small raw papaya, slice it paper-thin, and simmer the slivers in a light sugar syrup with a little lime juice and a pinch of panch phoron until translucent. It takes practice to get the slices even, which is why the tomato version is the everyday choice.
- **Aam-khejur:** in summer, when raw mangoes are in season, replace half the tomatoes with peeled raw mango.

## Storing

Cool completely, then spoon into a clean glass jar and refrigerate for up to a week. Bring to room temperature before serving. Do not freeze; the texture becomes watery.

## Serving

Serve a tablespoon or two per person at room temperature, near the end of the meal, with a papad or a last piece of beguni. It also goes well with [luchi](/durga-puja/recipes/luchi-alur-dom/) for breakfast.
