Labra is the humble, comforting partner to [bhoger khichuri](/durga-puja/recipes/bhoger-khichuri/). On the Durga Puja bhog plate it sits right next to the khichuri, a soft heap of pumpkin, potato, radish and greens that tastes gently sweet, a little hot and fragrant with *panch phoron*. Few people make it the same way twice, and that is part of its charm.

## Its place in the puja

At community pujas, labra is cooked in vast quantities on [Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) and often on Saptami and [Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/) as well, using whatever vegetables arrive at the puja kitchen that morning. It reflects an idea that runs through bhog cooking: simple, seasonal food, cooked with care and no onion or garlic, becomes fit for the goddess. Once offered, it is served as prasad alongside khichuri, [beguni](/durga-puja/recipes/beguni/) and [chutney](/durga-puja/recipes/tomato-khejur-chutney/). Our [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) guide covers the full thali.

## Tips for success

- **Cut evenly, cook in stages.** Hard vegetables such as potato and radish go in first, softer ones such as pumpkin and aubergine follow. This gives a labra where some pieces hold their shape and others melt.
- **Do not over-water.** Labra should be moist and saucy, not a soup. Start with half a cup of water and add a few spoons more only if the pan dries before the potato is cooked.
- **Stir gently.** Use a flat spoon and fold rather than churn, so the pumpkin does not turn to mash too soon.
- **Watch the fenugreek.** The fenugreek seeds in panch phoron turn bitter if they go dark brown. Keep the heat at medium once the oil has smoked.
- **Balance with sugar.** The small amount of sugar is traditional in Bengali vegetable dishes and brings out the pumpkin's sweetness. Measure it, since bhog is often not tasted before it is offered.
- **Smoke the mustard oil.** Heating mustard oil until it just smokes removes its raw edge. Keep a window open; it is pungent.

## Variations

- **Raw banana and cauliflower:** add peeled raw banana chunks with the potatoes, or cauliflower florets with the pumpkin.
- **Bori:** fry a handful of *bori* (sun-dried lentil dumplings) in the oil first, lift them out and stir them in at the end.
- **Coconut:** a few tablespoons of grated fresh coconut stirred in at the end make a richer, festive labra.
- **Hing:** add a pinch of asafoetida with the panch phoron for extra savouriness in a no-onion kitchen.
- **Greens:** pui shaak (Malabar spinach) is classic, but spinach, amaranth or chopped radish greens all work.

## Storing and reheating

Labra keeps in a covered container in the fridge for up to 2 days. Reheat gently in a pan with a splash of water, stirring carefully. It tastes even better the next day, though the vegetables will be softer. Prasad is generally best eaten fresh on the day of the puja.

## Serving

Serve warm, spooned beside hot khichuri, with a beguni and a little chutney on the side. Labra is also good with plain rice and dal, or with [luchi](/durga-puja/recipes/luchi-alur-dom/) for a festive breakfast.
