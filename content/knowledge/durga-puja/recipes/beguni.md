Beguni is what makes a plate of [bhoger khichuri](/durga-puja/recipes/bhoger-khichuri/) feel like a feast. The name comes from *begun*, the Bengali word for aubergine: slices of it are dipped in a thick, spiced gram-flour batter and fried until the outside is crisp and the inside soft and creamy. It takes very little time, but it needs to be eaten hot.

## Its place in the puja

Every Durga Puja bhog plate needs something crisp, and beguni is the most popular choice. At community puja kitchens on [Ashtami](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/) and [Navami](/durga-puja/rituals/navami/), one volunteer often does nothing but fry beguni for hours while others serve. Some pujas serve *alu bhaja* (fried potato), pumpkin fritters or papad instead. In a home puja, fry the beguni last, just before the bhog is offered, so it reaches the goddess and then the guests while still crisp. See the full plate in our [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) guide.

## Tips for success

- **Slice evenly.** Slices about 5 mm thick cook through in the time it takes the batter to brown. Thicker slices stay raw inside, thinner ones turn papery.
- **Dry the slices.** The salt draws out some moisture; pat the slices dry before dipping so the batter sticks.
- **Keep the batter thick.** A thin batter slides off and soaks up oil. It should cling in an even, opaque layer.
- **Use rice flour.** It is the simplest trick for a coating that stays crisp after frying.
- **Fry at a steady medium-high heat.** Too hot, and the coating browns before the aubergine softens; too cool, and the beguni turns greasy.
- **Mustard oil is traditional.** If using it, heat it until it just smokes, then bring it down to frying temperature. A neutral oil gives a milder result.

## Variations

- **Poppy seeds:** sprinkle white poppy seeds (*posto*) onto the battered slices before frying for extra crunch.
- **Other vegetables:** the same batter makes thin pumpkin slices, potato slices, cauliflower florets or *ponir* (paneer) fritters.
- **Spicier:** add ½ tsp ground cumin and finely chopped green chilli to the batter.
- **Shallow-fried:** for less oil, shallow-fry in 4 tbsp oil in a flat pan, 3 minutes a side. It will be less puffy but still good.

## Storing and reheating

Beguni is best eaten within 10–15 minutes of frying. Leftover batter keeps in the fridge for a day. If you must reheat beguni, crisp it on a tray in a hot oven at 200°C for 5–6 minutes rather than in a microwave, which makes it soft.

## Serving

Serve beguni with khichuri and [labra](/durga-puja/recipes/labra/), placing it on the side of the plate so it stays crisp. It is equally good with plain rice and dal, with [luchi](/durga-puja/recipes/luchi-alur-dom/), or on its own with a cup of tea and puffed rice (*muri*) on a rainy evening.
