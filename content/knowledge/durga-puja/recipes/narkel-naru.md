Narkel naru are the sweets that grandmothers make in batches after Durga Puja. When relatives visit to exchange Bijoya greetings after [Dashami](/durga-puja/rituals/dashami/), the plate brought out for them often holds a few of these small brown coconut balls, along with *nimki* (savoury fried crackers) and shop sweets. A few days later, at [Kojagari Lakshmi Puja](/festivals/kojagari-lakshmi-puja/), naru appear again on the offering plate. They need only coconut, jaggery and patience.

## Its place in the puja season

The days after Durga Puja are a time of visiting. After Bijoya Dashami, which falls on Wednesday 21 October in 2026, families call on elders, touch their feet, exchange embraces and share sweets. Home-made sweets carry particular warmth on these visits, and narkel naru, which keep well for several days, are a favourite to make ahead. At Kojagari Lakshmi Puja, the full-moon worship of the goddess of prosperity, many households offer naru, *moa* (puffed-rice balls) and *tiler naru* (sesame balls). Exactly which sweets are offered differs from family to family. Read more about the wider food traditions in our [Durga Puja food and bhog](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) guide.

## Tips for success

- **Fresh coconut is best.** Freshly grated coconut gives naru their soft, juicy bite. Choose a heavy coconut that sloshes when shaken.
- **Grate only the white flesh.** The brown inner skin darkens the naru and makes them gritty.
- **Choose good jaggery.** Soft, fragrant cane jaggery (*akher gur*) is the everyday choice. In winter, date-palm jaggery (*khejur gur* or *nolen gur*) gives a richer flavour, but Lakshmi Puja usually comes before its season.
- **Low heat and constant stirring.** Jaggery burns easily. Keep the heat low and scrape the bottom of the pan.
- **Know when to stop.** Undercooked mixture will not hold; overcooked mixture turns hard and crumbly. The "leaves the sides of the pan" stage plus the pinch test is your guide.
- **Roll while warm.** Shape the naru as soon as the mixture can be handled. If it cools too much, warm it gently for a minute.

## Variations

- **Sugar naru (white naru):** use 150 g sugar instead of jaggery for pale, sweeter naru, often made alongside the brown ones.
- **Khoya naru:** stir in 50 g crumbled khoya (reduced milk solids) in the last 5 minutes for a richer, softer naru.
- **Nuts:** press a sliver of pistachio or a raisin onto each ball.
- **Narkel chhapa:** press the mixture into small sweet moulds instead of rolling it, for patterned coconut sweets.

## Storing

Store the naru in an airtight container. In a cool room they keep for 3–4 days; in the fridge, up to 10 days. Bring them to room temperature before serving, as they soften slightly and taste better.

## Serving

Arrange naru on a plate with other sweets for Bijoya guests, or place them in a small brass or steel bowl on the Lakshmi Puja offering plate. They also make a good end to a festive meal alongside [payesh](/durga-puja/recipes/payesh/), and a thoughtful gift to take when visiting family during the puja season. Planning a home puja?
