The short answer: **go when the city is quieter, see fewer pandals closer together, and plan for being separated before it happens.** Durga Puja is a family festival, and children and grandparents are at the heart of it. But Kolkata's evening crowds, long queues, steps and heat can be hard on small children, older people and anyone with limited mobility. With the right timing and a few precautions, everyone can enjoy it. For general street tactics, see our [pandal hopping tips](/durga-puja/pandal-hopping-tips/); this page focuses on the needs of families.

## Timing is everything

Crowds follow a daily rhythm, and the difference between peak and quiet hours is enormous.

- **Early morning (dawn to about 9 am).** The calmest time, even at famous pandals. Cooler too. Our [Central Blockbusters at Dawn](/guide/trails/central_blockbusters/) trail is built for this.
- **Late morning to afternoon.** Moderate crowds, but hotter. A good time for heritage and family pujas, which have more space and often somewhere to sit.
- **Evening (about 6 pm to midnight), Saptami to Navami.** Peak hours. Best avoided with toddlers, wheelchairs or frail relatives, at least at the famous pandals.
- **Before Shashthi.** Many big pandals open to visitors around Panchami (Friday 16 October 2026) or earlier, when crowds are lighter. Opening days vary by committee.

In 2026 the main days are Shashthi, Saturday 17 October, to Bijoya Dashami, Wednesday 21 October; see the [full dates](/guide/dates/).

## Which pandals, and when

There's no permanently "quiet" pandal; it depends on the hour and the day. As a rule:

- **Big theme pandals** in central and south Kolkata, such as [Md. Ali Park](/guide/pandals/md_ali_park/) or [Ekdalia Evergreen](/guide/pandals/ekdalia/), are best in the early morning.
- **Heritage pujas in north Kolkata**, such as [Bagbazar](/guide/pandals/bagbazar/) and [Sovabazar Rajbari](/guide/pandals/sovabazar_rajbari/), are pleasant in the morning or afternoon. Read about [bonedi bari pujas](/durga-puja/bonedi-bari-pujas/).
- **Salt Lake's block pujas**, like [FD Block](/guide/pandals/fd_block/), sit on wider roads with more space to move. The [Salt Lake and Lake Town Hop](/guide/trails/east_hop/) suits families; see the [Salt Lake area guide](/guide/areas/salt_lake/).
- **Your own neighbourhood puja** is often the easiest of all: no travel, familiar faces and a place to sit.

The free [Pujo Parikrama guide](/guide/) shows each pandal's best visiting slot and **live crowd check-ins** from other visitors, so you can see which pandals are busy right now and change plans before you leave.

## A lost-child plan

Plan this before you leave home, not at the pandal.

- **Name tag or card.** Put your phone number (and a second adult's) on a card in a secure pocket, on a wristband or on the back of a name tag. Avoid displaying the child's name prominently where strangers can read it.
- **Photo.** Take a photo of each child that morning, so you can show what they are wearing.
- **The rule.** Teach children: if you're lost, stop, stay where you are, and go to a police officer, a volunteer in a committee badge or the announcement desk.
- **Meeting points.** Agree one at every pandal, such as the entry gate or a landmark.
- **Bright clothes.** A colourful top or cap makes a child easier to spot.
- **Hold hands** in queues and at entry and exit points, where crowds compress.

Police help desks are usually set up near large pandals and along main roads, and committees run announcement desks. If anyone goes missing, tell the nearest one immediately. India's national emergency number is 112.

## Strollers, carriers and getting around

- **Carriers and slings** are usually better than strollers in pandals, where entrances often have steps, narrow paths and dense queues, and some don't allow strollers inside.
- **A light folding stroller** helps on wide roads and for sleeping toddlers between pandals.
- **Metro.** Stations are crowded in the evenings; travel off-peak where you can, and use lifts where available. See the [Kolkata trip guide](/durga-puja/visit-kolkata/) for getting around.
- **Cars.** Roads near big pandals are often closed or jammed. Drop-offs a short walk away work better; see our [parking guide](/guide/parking/).

## Seating, rest and toilets

- **Plan rest stops**: a café, a park, a relative's home or a quiet family-puja courtyard.
- **Carry a lightweight folding stool** for older relatives if you expect to wait, but don't use it in queues or narrow lanes.
- **Ask about seating.** Some pujas have chairs near the altar or a seating area for older visitors, especially during aarti.
- **Toilets** near pandals are limited; plan visits around restaurants, malls or hotels.
- **Food and water.** Carry water and snacks; street food is a highlight, but choose busy stalls and keep a backup for fussy eaters. Our [food and bhog guide](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) has ideas.

## Accessibility realities

Being honest helps you plan. Many pandals are temporary structures with raised floors, steps at the entrance, cables underfoot, uneven or muddy ground and narrow one-way paths. Accessibility varies a great deal between pujas and from year to year.

- **Ask on arrival.** Some committees provide a separate entrance, ramp or priority line for elderly and disabled visitors; volunteers know.
- **Go at quiet hours**, when volunteers have time to help and crowds can make space.
- **Wheelchair users**: wider-road areas like Salt Lake and pujas in parks and open grounds are generally easier than narrow north Kolkata lanes.
- **Sensory needs**: the dhak, loudspeakers and crowds are loud. Ear defenders and a planned quiet break help children and adults with sensory sensitivities.
- **Visual or hearing impairment**: go with a companion and stay at the edge of the crowd flow.

## Medical needs

- Carry regular medicines, with a little extra, plus prescriptions if needed.
- Pack oral rehydration salts; heat and humidity drain energy quickly.
- Note the nearest hospital or clinic to each area you plan to visit.
- If someone feels faint, move them out of the flow and ask a volunteer or police help desk for first aid.
- People with heart, breathing or mobility conditions may prefer to avoid peak crowds entirely and enjoy the puja at a [family or home puja](/durga-puja/at-home/), or watch aarti from the edge.
