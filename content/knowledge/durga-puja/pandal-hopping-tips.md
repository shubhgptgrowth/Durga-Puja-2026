The short answer: **go early or go late, travel by Metro, walk in comfortable shoes, and plan one area at a time.** Pandal hopping, called *thakur dekha* ("seeing the goddess") in Bengali, is the heart of Durga Puja in Kolkata, and the city has hundreds of pandals spread over a large area. A little planning turns a night of queues and traffic into one of the best walks of your year. In 2026 the main puja days are Shashthi (Saturday 17 October) to Bijoya Dashami (Wednesday 21 October); see the full [2026 dates](/guide/dates/).

## Pick the right hours

Crowds follow a predictable daily rhythm.

- **Early morning (dawn to about 9 am).** The calmest time. Pandals are open, the light is soft, and you will often hear the morning puja and the [dhak](/durga-puja/dhak-and-dhaki/). Our [Central Blockbusters at Dawn](/guide/trails/central_blockbusters/) trail is built for this.
- **Midday and afternoon.** Moderate crowds and heat. A good time for heritage pujas, such as the [bonedi bari pujas](/durga-puja/bonedi-bari-pujas/) of north Kolkata.
- **Evening (about 6 pm to midnight).** Peak hours. The lights are at their best, but queues at famous pandals can be very long, especially from Saptami to Navami.
- **After about 1 am.** Crowds thin out noticeably, though the city stays awake. The [All-Nighter trail](/guide/trails/all_nighter/) runs north to south through the night.

Many big pandals open to visitors before Shashthi, often from Panchami (Friday 16 October 2026) or even earlier, which is a good time to see them with fewer people. Opening days vary by committee.

## Use the Metro and walk

Car travel near major pandals is slow, and many lanes are closed to vehicles in the evenings. The Kolkata Metro is the quickest way to move between clusters: stations such as Sovabazar, Shyambazar, MG Road, Central, Kalighat and Rabindra Sarobar are within walking distance of well-known pujas. Expect stations to be crowded at peak hours and check extended puja-time schedules locally.

If you do drive, park once and walk. Our [parking guide](/guide/parking/) lists car parks near each area, and each area page shows the nearest Metro station.

## What to carry

- **Comfortable shoes.** You may walk 10 km or more in a night. Wear shoes you have already broken in; new festive footwear is best kept for one or two pandals.
- **Water.** Carry a bottle and refill it; queues can be long and humid.
- **Phone and power bank.** Maps, UPI and calls drain batteries fast in crowds.
- **Cash and UPI.** Many stalls accept UPI, but networks get congested in dense crowds; keep small notes as backup.
- **A small bag that zips.** Keep valuables in front pockets or a cross-body bag.
- **Basics.** Tissues, a light scarf or umbrella (October can still bring rain), and any personal medicines.

## Kids and elderly visitors

- Put a card with your phone number in each child's pocket, and agree on a meeting point at every pandal.
- Choose morning or afternoon visits with children and older relatives, and keep the list short.
- Avoid the narrowest entry lanes at peak time; ask volunteers whether a separate entrance exists for elderly or differently abled visitors. Some committees provide one, but not all.
- Plan rest stops: a café, a park or a quiet bonedi bari courtyard helps.

## Safety and help

- **Police help desks and control rooms** are usually set up near large pandals and along main roads. They help with lost children, lost phones and first aid. Pandal committees also run their own volunteer and announcement desks.
- Follow barricades and volunteers' directions; queues move in one direction for a reason.
- Do not stop to take photos on stairways or in narrow entry and exit paths.
- If you feel unwell in a crowd, move to the side and ask a volunteer for help.
- Keep emergency numbers saved, and share your live location with a friend if you split up.

## Respect at the pandal

A pandal is a place of worship. Remove shoes where asked, don't touch the protima (idol) or ritual items, keep flash and selfie sticks away from the altar during puja and aarti, and don't block others' view for long. Read the [glossary](/durga-puja/glossary/) to understand what you're seeing.

Kolkata's pujas cluster into neighbourhoods with distinct personalities. Plan one or two neighbouring areas per outing.

## Ready-made trails

If you'd rather not plan, follow a trail:

- [North Kolkata Heritage Trail](/guide/trails/north_heritage/): Kumartuli, Bagbazar and the old family pujas.
- [Central Blockbusters at Dawn](/guide/trails/central_blockbusters/): the giant theme pandals before the crowds wake up.
- [South Heavyweights](/guide/trails/south_classic/): the classic south Kolkata loop.
- [Behala and New Alipore Theme Trail](/guide/trails/behala_trail/): theme pujas of the southwest.
- [Salt Lake and Lake Town Hop](/guide/trails/east_hop/): the eastern side, good for families.
- [The All-Nighter](/guide/trails/all_nighter/): north to south through the night.

## Eat along the way

Plan food stops into your route: phuchka, rolls and mughlai paratha are everywhere, and old cabins near the pandals make good rest stops. Our [Durga Puja food guide](/durga-puja/food-and-bhog/) explains bhog and the classic pujo snacks.
