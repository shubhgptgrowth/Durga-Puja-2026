A **bonedi bari puja** (বনেদি বাড়ির পুজো, "the puja of an established house") is a Durga Puja held by one of Kolkata's old families in its own ancestral home. Instead of a themed pandal on the street, you step into a courtyard surrounded by verandas, and at one end, on a raised, pillared platform called the *thakur dalan*, stands the goddess with her children in a single traditional frame. The puja is run the way the family has run it for generations, sometimes for well over two centuries. Many of these houses are in north Kolkata, and several welcome respectful visitors.

## What makes a bonedi bari puja different

### The thakur dalan

The *thakur dalan* is a worship hall built into the house, usually facing a central courtyard, with columns, arches and often decorative plasterwork. It was designed for exactly this puja: the goddess sits in the hall, the family and guests gather in the courtyard, and the dhakis play nearby. Some thakur dalans are architecturally striking, a reminder of the wealth of the families who built them in colonial Calcutta.

### Ekchala idols and old forms

Most bonedi bari idols are **ekchala** (একচালা), with Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartik in one frame under a single arched backdrop. Families typically keep to the same form year after year: the same style of face, the same colours, the same *daker saaj* (silver-foil decoration) or *sholar saaj* (white pith decoration). In several houses the image is made in the courtyard itself, by artisans whose families have worked for that house for generations.

### Family rituals

Rituals follow the family's own customs, and these vary a great deal:

- Each house keeps its own *panjika* (almanac) traditions and order of rites, guided by its family *purohit* (priest).
- Bhog may follow old family recipes, and some houses are known for particular offerings such as sweets made at home.
- In some old families animal sacrifice (*bali*) was once part of the puja; many have long since replaced it with symbolic offerings such as ash gourd or sugarcane, or stopped the practice. Customs differ, so don't assume any one house's practice.
- Some families perform [Kumari Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/kumari-puja/) or hold their own immersion customs on [Dashami](/durga-puja/rituals/dashami/).

### Atmosphere

Compared with the big sarbojanin (community) pandals, bonedi bari pujas are quieter and more intimate. You see the puja as a household event: relatives back from abroad, elders seated in the verandas, children running around, and the priest at work a few metres away. Many visitors come for exactly this feeling of an older Calcutta.

## Well-known bonedi bari pujas

These are among the most widely known family pujas in Kolkata. Timings, access and customs change, so check locally before you go.

- **Sovabazar Rajbari** (north Kolkata). The puja is traditionally said to have been started in 1757 by Raja Nabakrishna Deb, and popular accounts link its first celebration to the aftermath of the Battle of Plassey. The family's branches hold pujas in the old palace complex, whose thakur dalan is among the most photographed in the city. See [Sovabazar Rajbari in our guide](/guide/pandals/sovabazar_rajbari/).
- **Rani Rashmoni's family house, Janbazar** (central Kolkata). The Durga Puja of the house associated with Rani Rashmoni, the nineteenth-century landowner and philanthropist who founded the Dakshineswar temple, is one of the best known family pujas in the city.
- **Laha Bari** (north Kolkata). The Laha family, an old merchant family, is known for its long-running puja.
- **Mallick Bari.** Several branches of the Mallick family hold old pujas, including the well-known Mallick Bari puja in Bhowanipore.
- **Chhatu Babu–Latu Babu Bari** (north Kolkata). The puja of the house associated with the sons of the merchant Ramdulal Dey, popularly known as Chhatu Babu and Latu Babu.
- **Dawn Bari, Jorasanko** (north Kolkata). The Dawn family puja is especially known for the ornaments and decoration of its goddess.
- **Sabarna Roy Choudhury family puja** (Barisha, Behala). Often cited as one of the oldest Durga Pujas in the Kolkata area, commonly dated to the early seventeenth century.

Many other family pujas continue across north and south Kolkata and in the districts, some well known and some known only to their neighbourhoods.

## How to visit

The easiest way is to combine bonedi baris with the old community pujas of north Kolkata. Our [North Kolkata Heritage Trail](/guide/trails/north_heritage/) starts near Sovabazar Metro and passes Kumartuli and Bagbazar; the [north Kolkata area guide](/guide/areas/north/) lists more pujas, food and parking nearby. See [Kumartuli idol-making](/durga-puja/kumartuli-idol-making/) for the artisans' quarter on the same walk.

- **When:** late morning to afternoon, Saptami (Sunday 18 October 2026) to Navami (Tuesday 20 October 2026), is usually best.
- **Getting there:** Metro to Sovabazar or Shyambazar for most north Kolkata houses; lanes are narrow and parking is scarce.

## Visiting etiquette

You are a guest in someone's home and place of worship.

- **Ask before entering** if there is no clear visitor entry, and follow the family's or volunteers' directions.
- **Dress modestly** and remove shoes where asked, especially near the thakur dalan.
- **Don't climb onto the thakur dalan** or touch the idol, ritual items or bhog unless invited.
- **Photography:** ask first; some families restrict it, particularly during rituals. Avoid flash.
- **Keep your voice down** during puja, aarti and anjali, and step aside for family members performing rites.
- **Don't treat private rooms as part of the visit.** Only the courtyard and areas opened to guests are for visitors.
- If you are offered prasad or bhog, accept it with thanks.

To understand what you're seeing, keep our [glossary](/durga-puja/glossary/) handy, and read the [history of Durga Puja](/durga-puja/history/) for how these family pujas shaped the festival before community pujas took over the streets.
