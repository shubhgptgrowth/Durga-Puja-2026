For Bengalis, Durga Puja is the high point of a season, not a single festival. It begins with **Vishwakarma Puja** in mid-September, peaks with the five days of Durga Puja, and carries on through **Kojagari Lakshmi Puja**, **Kali Puja** with Diwali, **Bhai Phonta** and **Jagaddhatri Puja** until late autumn. In spring, **Basanti Puja** brings Durga back in the month of Chaitra.

Almost all of these follow the lunar calendar, so their dates shift from year to year. The only dates this guide states as fact are those of Durga Puja 2026, which ends with Bijoya Dashami on **Wednesday 21 October 2026**; for the rest, check your local panjika (almanac) or our [dates page](/guide/dates/).

## The season at a glance

| Festival | When it falls | What it celebrates |
|---|---|---|
| Vishwakarma Puja | Usually 17 September (solar calendar) | The divine architect; tools, machines and workshops |
| Durga Puja | Shashthi to Dashami, bright fortnight of Ashwin | The goddess's homecoming and victory over Mahishasura |
| Kojagari Lakshmi Puja | Full moon (Purnima) of Ashwin, a few days after Dashami | Lakshmi, goddess of prosperity, in the home |
| Kali Puja and Diwali | New moon (Amavasya) of Kartik | Kali, worshipped through the night; lamps everywhere |
| Bhai Phonta | Second lunar day after the Kartik new moon | Sisters praying for their brothers' long life |
| Jagaddhatri Puja | Ninth lunar day (Navami) of Kartik's bright fortnight | Jagaddhatri, "holder of the world" |
| Basanti Puja | Bright fortnight of Chaitra (spring) | Durga worshipped in her "original" season |

## Before Durga Puja: Vishwakarma Puja

Vishwakarma, the divine craftsman and architect, is worshipped in factories, workshops, garages and offices, with tools and machines cleaned and garlanded. In Kolkata the day is famous for kite flying from rooftops. For many Bengalis it is the first sign that the Pujas are close. Read more about [Vishwakarma Puja](/festivals/vishwakarma-puja/).

## Durga Puja itself

The season's centre runs from [Mahalaya](/durga-puja/rituals/mahalaya/), which opens Devi Paksha, through Shashthi, Saptami, Ashtami and Navami to [Bijoya Dashami](/durga-puja/rituals/dashami/). If you are new to the festival, start with [what Durga Puja is](/durga-puja/what-is-durga-puja/) and the [rituals day by day](/durga-puja/rituals/).

## Kojagari Lakshmi Puja

On the full moon after Dashami, many Bengali homes worship Lakshmi with alpana (rice-paste floor designs), small footprints drawn leading into the house, and a simple bhog of khichuri and naru. The name comes from *ko jagarti*, "who is awake?", the question Lakshmi is said to ask as she visits homes that night. See [Kojagari Lakshmi Puja](/festivals/kojagari-lakshmi-puja/).

## Kali Puja and Diwali

On the new moon night of Kartik, while much of India celebrates Diwali, Bengal worships Kali, often through the night, with lamps on every ledge and fireworks in the streets. The evening before is Bhoot Chaturdashi, with fourteen lamps and fourteen leafy greens. Read about [Kali Puja and Diwali](/festivals/kali-puja-diwali/).

## Bhai Phonta

Two days after Kali Puja, sisters put a *phonta* (forehead mark) of sandal paste, kajal and curd on their brothers and pray for their long life, followed by a large family meal and gifts. It is Bengal's version of Bhai Dooj. See [Bhai Phonta](/festivals/bhai-phonta/).

## Jagaddhatri Puja

On the ninth lunar day of Kartik's bright fortnight, Jagaddhatri, a form of Durga riding a lion that stands over an elephant, is worshipped. The festival is biggest in Chandannagar, known for its light art, and in Krishnanagar, which tradition links to Raja Krishnachandra of Nadia. Read about [Jagaddhatri Puja in Chandannagar](/festivals/jagaddhatri-puja-chandannagar/).

## In spring: Basanti Puja

Basanti Puja is Durga Puja in the month of Chaitra. In the Puranic account, spring was the season of Durga's worship; the autumn festival recalls Rama's [akal bodhon](/durga-puja/akal-bodhon/), the "untimely awakening". Basanti Puja is kept by a number of families and temples and coincides with Chaitra Navratri. See [Basanti Puja](/festivals/basanti-puja/).

## Planning around the season

If you are visiting Kolkata, the city stays festive long after Dashami: Bijoya visits continue until Lakshmi Puja, Kali Puja pandals rise within three weeks, and Chandannagar's Jagaddhatri lights draw crowds from across the region.
