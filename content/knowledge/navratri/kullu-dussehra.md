**Kullu Dussehra** turns the usual calendar around. When the rest of north India burns Ravana's effigy on Vijayadashami and goes home, the Kullu valley in Himachal Pradesh is just beginning: for a week, the gods and goddesses of hundreds of mountain villages are carried down to **Dhalpur Maidan** in Kullu town to pay homage to **Raghunath**, the valley's form of Rama. It has been recognised as an international festival since the 1970s and draws very large crowds every year.

## Kullu Dussehra 2026 dates

The festival starts on **Vijayadashami** and lasts **about seven days**. Most 2026 calendars place Vijayadashami on **Tuesday 20 October**, which would put the festival at roughly **20 to 26 October**. Kullu fixes its own dates, and some travel listings show other dates, so confirm with the Kullu district administration or Himachal Pradesh tourism before you travel.

## How it began

The story is usually told like this: in the 17th century, Raja Jagat Singh of Kullu, seeking to atone for a wrong, had an idol of Raghunath brought from Ayodhya, installed it as the valley's ruling deity, and declared himself its servant. Dussehra became the great annual festival of Raghunath, and the local deities of the valley, each with its own village, temple and attendants, came to attend on him. The royal family's descendants still play their part in the rituals.

## What happens

**The first day: the rath yatra.** Raghunath's idol is brought to Dhalpur Maidan and placed on a wooden chariot. Thousands of people take hold of thick ropes and pull the chariot across the ground, with the village deities in attendance, drums and long horns (*narsingha* and *karnal*) sounding, and the crowd shouting with joy.

**The gathering of the deities.** Each village deity travels in a decorated palanquin (*palki* or *rath*) carried on the shoulders of its villagers, with its own musicians and flags. Through the week the deities sit in camps around the ground, receive visitors, and "visit" one another. Many people come simply to see so many local gods in one place.

**The fair.** Dhalpur becomes a huge market of shawls, woollens, local produce and food, with folk dance performances in the evenings, including the Himachali *naati* circle dance, and cultural troupes from other states and countries.

**The last day: Lanka Dahan.** The festival ends on the banks of the Beas river, where a pile representing Lanka is burned in a symbolic ritual. Raghunath returns to his temple, and the village deities begin the long walk home.

## Practical tips

- The nearest airport is **Bhuntar**, about 10 km from Kullu; most visitors come by road from Chandigarh or Delhi.
- Book rooms in Kullu or nearby Manali well ahead for the first day.
- Be at Dhalpur early on the first day to see the chariot pulled; the crowd is thickest in the afternoon.
- Ask before photographing a deity's palanquin up close, and follow the attendants' lead.
