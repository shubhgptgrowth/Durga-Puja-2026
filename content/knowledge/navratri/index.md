**Navratri** (also written Navaratri) means "nine nights". It is a Hindu festival in which the Goddess is worshipped for nine nights, most widely as the **Navadurga**, nine forms of Durga, followed by a tenth day of victory, **Vijayadashami** or **Dussehra**. In 2026, **Sharad Navratri begins after Mahalaya** (Saturday 10 October 2026). Per the Bengal calendar, **Maha Ashtami is Monday 19 October**, **Maha Navami is Tuesday 20 October** and **Vijayadashami is Wednesday 21 October 2026**.

Bengal's **Durga Puja** falls in exactly the same season and honours the same Goddess, but it looks very different on the ground. This page explains what Navratri is, how it is observed across India, and how it relates to the Durga Puja you will see in Kolkata.

## What Navratri celebrates

The festival honours *Shakti*, the divine feminine power. Its best-known story comes from the *Devi Mahatmya*, in which the Goddess, formed from the combined energies of the gods, battles the buffalo demon **Mahishasura** and defeats him. Many people also connect the festival with Rama's worship of the Goddess before his battle with Ravana, which is why the tenth day celebrates Rama's victory in much of north India.

Across traditions, the nine nights are read as a journey: from the Goddess's fierce, protective forms towards her forms of wisdom and fulfilment, and from inner darkness towards light.

## How many Navratris are there?

There are four Navratris in the Hindu year, each in the bright fortnight of a lunar month:

| Navratri | Season | Lunar month | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chaitra (Vasanta) Navratri | Spring, March–April | Chaitra | Ram Navami |
| Ashadha Gupt Navratri | Monsoon, June–July | Ashadha | Observed mainly by tantric and Shakta practitioners |
| Sharad Navratri | Autumn, September–October | Ashvin | Vijayadashami / Dussehra |
| Magha Gupt Navratri | Winter, January–February | Magha | Observed mainly by tantric and Shakta practitioners |

**Sharad Navratri** is the one most people mean when they say "Navratri", and it is the one that coincides with Durga Puja. **Chaitra Navratri** is also widely observed, especially in north India, and its ninth day is the birthday of Rama.

## Navratri 2026 dates

- **Mahalaya / Sarva Pitru Amavasya:** Saturday 10 October 2026. This new moon closes *Pitru Paksha*, the fortnight for remembering ancestors. Sharad Navratri begins after it.
- **First day (Pratipada) and ghatasthapana:** check your local panchang. The exact day and the auspicious window for setting up the kalash depend on when the tithi begins where you live and on your tradition.
- **Maha Ashtami (Durga Ashtami):** Monday 19 October 2026, per the Bengal calendar.
- **Maha Navami:** Tuesday 20 October 2026, per the Bengal calendar.
- **Vijayadashami / Dussehra:** Wednesday 21 October 2026, per the Bengal calendar.

Lunar days (*tithis*) do not line up neatly with calendar days. A tithi can start in the afternoon, run into the next morning, or occasionally be skipped or repeated, so north Indian, Gujarati, south Indian and Bengali almanacs can differ by a day. That is normal, not an error. For Bengal's day-by-day schedule, see [Durga Puja 2026 dates](/guide/dates/).

## The core rituals of Navratri

- **[Ghatasthapana](/navratri/ghatasthapana/)** (kalash sthapana): on the first day, a water-filled pot is installed as the seat of the Goddess, often beside a tray of soil sown with barley that sprouts over the nine days.
- **[Worship of the Navadurga](/navratri/navadurga/):** each day is dedicated to one form, from Shailaputri on the first day to Siddhidatri on the ninth.
- **Akhand jyoti:** many homes keep a lamp burning continuously for the nine days.
- **[Fasting](/navratri/fasting-rules/):** from a full fast to one meal a day or fruit and milk only, avoiding grains, onion and garlic.
- **Recitation:** the *Durga Saptashati* (Chandi Path) or devotional songs and *jagrata* (night-long singing).
- **[Kanya pujan](/navratri/kanya-pujan/):** on Ashtami or Navami, young girls are honoured as forms of the Goddess and fed.
- **[Garba and dandiya](/navratri/garba-dandiya/):** in Gujarat and now across India and abroad, communities dance in circles every night.
- **Golu / Bommai Kolu:** in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, families arrange tiered displays of dolls and invite neighbours.
- **Ayudha Puja and Saraswati Puja:** in the south, tools, books and instruments are worshipped on Navami.

## Navratri and Durga Puja: same season, different practices

| | Navratri (much of India) | Durga Puja (Bengal and eastern India) |
|---|---|---|
| Main days | Nine nights from Pratipada | Five days, Shashthi to Dashami (festivities start from Mahalaya) |
| Image | Kalash, picture or small murti; Navadurga worshipped day by day | Large clay idol of Durga slaying Mahishasura, with Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartik |
| Where | Mostly homes and temples; garba grounds | Community pandals, family homes, streets |
| Key moments | Ghatasthapana, Ashtami, kanya pujan, Navami havan | [Bodhon](/durga-puja/rituals/shashthi/), [Kola Bou](/durga-puja/rituals/kola-bou/), [Ashtami anjali](/durga-puja/rituals/ashtami/), [Sandhi Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/sandhi-puja/), [Kumari Puja](/durga-puja/rituals/kumari-puja/) |
| Food | Fasting food, no grains, onion or garlic | Bhog khichuri; feasting, often including fish and meat on later days |
| Ending | Dussehra, Ravan dahan, Ramlila | Bijoya Dashami, [sindoor khela](/durga-puja/rituals/sindoor-khela/), immersion |

In Bengal the Goddess is understood as a daughter, Uma, returning to her parents' home with her children for a few days, which is why the mood of Dashami is a tender farewell. In much of north and west India, the emphasis is on austerity, devotion and the triumph of good over evil. Both are expressions of the same reverence, and many families, especially in cities like Kolkata, Delhi and Mumbai, now enjoy both: a pandal visit in the evening and garba later at night. Read more in [what is Durga Puja](/durga-puja/what-is-durga-puja/).

## How other communities celebrate

The nine nights look different in every part of India. Tamil, Telugu and Kannada homes set up [Golu](/navratri/golu/), steps of dolls visited by neighbours each evening; Telangana's women build flower towers for [Bathukamma](/navratri/bathukamma/); Karnataka holds the [Mysuru Dasara](/navratri/mysuru-dasara/) with its elephant procession; the Kullu valley gathers its village gods for [Kullu Dussehra](/navratri/kullu-dussehra/); and the south worships tools and books on Navami and starts children on their letters on Vijayadashami ([Ayudha Puja and Vidyarambham](/navratri/ayudha-puja-vidyarambham/)). See the whole map in [Navratri across India](/navratri/across-india/), and if you live in Kolkata, [Navratri in Kolkata](/navratri/in-kolkata/) for Ravan dahan, garba nights and fasting during Pujo.

## The tenth day

The festival closes with **[Vijayadashami](/navratri/dussehra-vijayadashami/)**, "the victorious tenth". In north India, effigies of Ravana are burned after Ramlila performances; in Mysuru, a royal procession marks Dasara; in Bengal, the idols are immersed and people exchange [Bijoya greetings](/durga-puja/bijoya-dashami-wishes/). The barley sprouts from ghatasthapana are often given as blessings or worn on the ear or in the turban on this day.
